# FADE solver report (Phase 0)

Generated by `npm run solve` on 2026-11-02 (raw numbers: `tools/solver/out/results.json`).
Rules as in PRD §2. Deliberate timeouts are a legal action, and the state includes each player's consecutive-timeout counter (0–2).

> **Simplifications.** The solver ignores the 30-ply sudden-death and 60-ply cap counters. Positions that never resolve to a forced win or loss (cycles) are scored as **draws**.
>
> This is safe in practice. The longest forced result anywhere on 3×3 is 18 plies (a 17-ply win or an 18-ply loss), so the cap can only cut short a forced win in a round already past ply 42.

## TL;DR

| Board | Perfect-play result | How deep | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3×3, K3, L3** | **First player wins** in 13 plies (their 7th mark) | Only an **edge** opening wins. After that, exactly **one** winning move every turn. Centre and corner openings are draws. | **Keep (recommended)** |
| 4×4, K4, L3 | First player wins in **5 plies** (3 marks) | Trivial double threat | Reject: shallower than 3×3 |
| 4×4, K4, L4 | No forced win for either side within 19–20 plies (time-boxed) | Random games hit the 60-ply cap **73%** of the time | Reject for v1: slow, drawish, and no perfect bot |

### Opening moves (first player)

```
 draw │ WIN  │ draw          Only the 4 edge cells win (in 13 plies).
──────┼──────┼──────         Centre and corners are draws with best play.
 WIN  │ draw │ WIN           Timing out on move 1 loses in 14.
──────┼──────┼──────
 draw │ WIN  │ draw
```

How narrow the winning line is, at each of the winner's turns (defender plays the slowest loss):

| Winner's mark # | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Winning actions / legal actions | 4/10 | 1/8 | 1/6 | 1/4 | 1/4 | 1/4 | 1/4 |

The win is real, but it needs 7 precise moves in a row on an 8 s clock (3 s in Blitz), starting with a counter-intuitive edge opening. That is the "simple to learn, hard to master" shape we want.

### Do deliberate timeouts matter?

Yes. Of the 66,682 positions reachable without timeouts, **3,420 (5.1%) change value** once a deliberate timeout is allowed, and every change is **draw → win**. Waiting out the clock to drop your oldest mark without placing is a genuine tempo weapon. The bot table includes it, so Hard and Impossible will sometimes do it on purpose.

## 4×4 (time-boxed, not solved)

Each 4×4 position space has up to **5.88 billion** slots (654 M mark arrangements × 9 counter states), too big to solve exactly in JS. Instead, each board got two runs:
- An iterative-deepening proof search with a 5-minute budget.
- 3,000 random playouts (random placements). A "tactical blunder" is a move that skips an immediate win, or lets the opponent force a win within 3 plies.

| | 4×4, K4, L3 | 4×4, K4, L4 |
|---|---|---|
| Proof search | **First player forces a win in 5 plies** | No forced win for X within 19 plies, nor for O within 20 (56.9 M nodes, budget hit at depth 21) |
| Avg branching (random games) | 11.7 | 9.7 |
| Avg random game length | 12.9 plies | 52.5 plies |
| Random games reaching the 60-ply cap | ~0% | **73%** |
| Tactical blunder rate (random move) | 65% | 11% |

For comparison, the same tactical-blunder measure on 3×3 is 40%; the exact mistake rate is 62%. All 4×4 numbers are **sampled or depth-limited**, not exact.

## Recommendation: keep 3×3, K3, L3

1. **4×4 L3 is broken.** The first player makes an open double threat with 3 marks.
2. **4×4 L4 is the opposite problem.** Four in a row with only four marks is easy to block. Random play rarely ends before the cap, and it is very forgiving (11% blunder rate). On a 3–8 s shot clock it would feel like shuffling, not dueling. There is no perfect-play table either, so the Hard and Impossible bots would need heuristic search, and "Impossible" couldn't be guaranteed.
3. **3×3 is sharp and deep for humans.**
   - It is a first-player win, but only by an unintuitive edge opening followed by a single correct move every turn. Most other moves throw it away: a random move is a mistake 62% of the time.
   - Rounds resolve in about 13 plies, which fits the shot clock and the 30-ply sudden death.
   - Connect Four is a solved first-player win too, and people still enjoy it.
4. **The match format already balances the first-move edge.** The starter alternates each round.

### What a forced first-player win means for the product

Raise these with Sarath:
- **Impossible bot:** when it starts a round it can never lose. When you start, you win only by playing the edge opening and then every move perfectly; otherwise it draws or beats you. That matches the "Impossible" label. Hard (10% second-best) is beatable, because on this narrow line the second-best move is usually a drawing one.
- **Perfect players:** between two perfect players, every round goes to its starter, so a best-of-5 would always end 3–2 to whoever started round 1 (chosen at random). Humans won't hit that ceiling in v1, but if it matters later, round 5 could be started by the player who lost round 4, or chosen by a fresh coin flip.
- **Onboarding hint:** "Opening in the centre feels strong… it isn't." That's a nice "aha" for later tips or puzzles.
